HAIRCUT STANDARDS

Review the applicable Army grooming standards and understand how they apply to Alpha Xuts haircut requests during AIT.

REGULATION

AR 670-1

What it covers

Army Regulation 670-1 sets the standards for the wear and appearance of Army uniforms and insignia, including requirements for a Soldier's hair and grooming. It's the source regulation behind every haircut rule referenced on this page.

This page gives a simplified, easy-to-read summary — it isn't a substitute for the regulation itself or for the guidance your AIT chain of command gives you.

↗ Search AR 670-1 on Army Pubs
SUMMARY

THE STANDARD

Military haircuts must maintain a neat, professional appearance and comply with Army grooming requirements. In practice, that means:

→

Hair must be neat and well groomed at all times.

→

Hair must not interfere with the proper wear and fit of required headgear or protective equipment.

→

Hair must maintain the required appearance around the ears and neckline.

→

Extreme or prohibited hairstyles are not permitted, regardless of phase.

→

Your specific AIT unit may set stricter requirements than the general Army standard — local policy always applies on top of this.

AIT HAIRCUT REQUESTS
AR 670-1 · SECTION 3-2

HAIRCUT REGULATIONS

A plain-language summary of the Army's hair and grooming standards that apply to haircuts. This is a simplified explanation, not the regulation itself.

General Principle

Hairstyles are judged first by whether a Soldier can still properly wear all required headgear (patrol cap, beret, helmet) and protective equipment. Hair that keeps headgear from fitting snugly, causes bulging, or interferes with a helmet or protective mask is not allowed. Styles that create a health or safety hazard are also prohibited.

Neat, Conservative, Non-Extreme

  • Hair must always look neat, clean, and well-maintained — never ragged, unkempt, or extreme.
  • Faddish or eccentric cuts and styles aren't authorized.
  • No shapes, lines, or designs cut into the hair or scalp.
  • If hair is dyed, it has to stay a natural color — bright, neon, or unnatural shades (blue, purple, green, fire-engine red, etc.) are not permitted.

Male Haircut Standard

  • Hair on top must be neatly groomed, without excessive length or bulk, and present a tapered look — meaning the outline follows the shape of the head and narrows in toward the base of the neck.
  • When combed, hair should not fall over the ears or eyebrows, or touch the collar (aside from closely-cut hair at the very back of the neck).
  • A moderate amount of block-cut fullness in the back is fine as long as the overall tapered shape is kept.
  • Fully shaved or very closely-cropped hair (like a 0 guard) is authorized.
  • Styles that leave a single untapered patch of hair — mohawks, "horseshoe" cuts, or a teardrop patch on the front — are considered eccentric and are not authorized.
  • Braids, cornrows, twists, dreadlocks, or locks are not authorized for male haircuts in uniform or on duty.

Sideburns

Sideburns can't extend below the bottom of the ear opening, can't be shaped to taper, flare, or come to a point, and individual hairs can't exceed 1/8 inch when fully extended.

Female Hairstyles

Female hair is grouped into short, medium, and long length categories, each with its own length and bulk limits, plus rules for bangs, buns, ponytails, braids, cornrows, twists, and locks. The same "neat, conservative, not faddish" principle applies throughout. Because the specifics vary by category, refer to the official regulation or your cadre for exact measurements.

This is a simplified, plain-language summary for Alpha Xuts and does not replace AR 670-1, other Army regulations, or local AIT policy. For the authoritative text, search AR 670-1 at armypubs.army.mil or check with your chain of command.
